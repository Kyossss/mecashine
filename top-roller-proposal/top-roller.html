Top polisher, before and after

The carriage's legs grew 15 mm so the roller hangs lower, a 608ZZ replaced the printed pin, the roller was rebuilt like the side brushes with 52 mm chenille yarn, and the ToF sensor moved out in front on a longer arm. Switch between Before and After to watch the same shoe pass under each.

Before and after

BeforeAfter

Before, the roller had no brush modelled; the simulation assumed a 50 mm brush. Parked figures are small / medium / large shoe, with the carriage at the top of its racks for the measuring pass.

What changed

  • Carriage: the far wall and the motor-side mount reach 15 mm lower. The top plate, lift brackets, pinion shaft, racks and the carriage's travel are unchanged.
  • Bearing: the 8 mm printed pin on the far wall is gone; a 608ZZ sits in a 22 mm pocket there and the roller's idler end turns in it on an 8 mm axle.
  • Motor holder: unchanged, it bolts on 15 mm lower.
  • Roller: the side cores' drive end on your D-shaped motor shaft, a 109 mm hex bar, 12 tie discs and an idler end. 78 strands of chenille, 52 mm across when spinning, 18 mm under the top plate.
  • ToF sensor: still in front of the roller, now 60 mm before its axis and 20 mm higher, so the beam's 25° cone stays 10.5 mm clear of the spinning yarn. The bracket mounts like the old one, a tab under the top plate into its two blind holes.
