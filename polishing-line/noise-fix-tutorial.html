Stop the brush motors from resetting the distance sensors

When the noisetest command shows silent readings, a VL53L0X stopped answering while a brush motor ran. The noise from the motors reset it. The five fixes below are in cost order, cheapest first. Do one, run noisetest again, and stop as soon as every row shows 0 silent.

What happens

A brushed motor makes a small spark at its brushes every time it turns. It also draws a surge of current when it starts or meets the shoe.

How it reaches the sensor

The sparks travel along the motor wires and jump into nearby cables. The current surges also shake the ground that the sensor shares with the motors.

What the sensor does

The sensor sees its 3.3 V supply dip, so it resets and stops answering on I2C. The firmware counts that as a silent reading.

1

100 nF across each motor

  1. Unplug the 12 V supply.
  2. Bend the capacitor's legs so the body sits close to the back of the motor.
  3. Solder one leg to each motor tab, on top of the existing wire joints. A ceramic capacitor has no polarity.
  4. Trim the spare leg length so it can't touch the motor case.
  5. Repeat on all three motors: side brush L, side brush R and the top roller.

This shorts out the spark noise right where it starts, before it can travel down the wires. It is the cheapest fix and usually the one that helps most.

3 × 100 nF ceramic (104), 50 V

If a motor is still noisy, add two more 100 nF capacitors on it: one from each tab to the metal case. Solder to the case quickly, or clamp the leg under the gearbox screw.

2

Bulk capacitor on each driver

  1. Unplug the 12 V supply.
  2. Find the stripe on the capacitor's can. That leg is negative, and it is also the shorter leg.
  3. Solder the negative leg to the driver's GND pin, the one right next to VM.
  4. Solder the positive leg to VM.
  5. Keep both legs short. Solder on the underside of the perfboard if there is no room on top.
  6. Fit one on TB6612 #1 and one on TB6612 #2.

Check the polarity twice. An electrolytic capacitor fitted backwards across 12 V gets hot and can burst. A 16 V capacitor is too close to 12 V, so use 25 V or higher.

The capacitor acts as a small local energy store. When a motor starts or presses on the shoe, it supplies the surge, so the 12 V line and the ground don't sag.

2 × 470–1000 µF electrolytic, 25 V+
3

Two capacitors at each sensor

  1. Unplug the 12 V supply.
  2. At the sensor, find where the cable's VIN and GND wires land. That can be the header pins on the back of the board or the connector on the cable.
  3. Solder the 100 nF ceramic across VIN and GND. It has no polarity.
  4. Solder the 10 µF across the same two points, with the stripe leg on GND.
  5. Cover the joints with heat shrink so they can't short against the bracket.
  6. Do the same on the side sensor and the top sensor.

The sensor's supply comes down a long cable, which picks up noise along the way. These two capacitors smooth it right at the sensor. The 10 µF covers slow dips and the 100 nF covers fast spikes.

2 × 10 µF, 16 V+2 × 100 nF ceramic (104)
4

Twist the motor wires and separate them

  1. Unsolder or unplug one end of a motor's red and black wires.
  2. Twist them together evenly, about one turn every 2–3 cm. Clamping one end and spinning the other with a drill set to slow works well.
  3. Reconnect the wires, then do the same for all three motors.
  4. Route the twisted pairs on a different path from the sensor cables. Aim for 5 cm apart or more.
  5. Where they must cross, cross them at a right angle. Don't run them side by side.

This matters most on the side jaws. The side sensor and both side motors ride on the moving racks, so their cables travel together. If they share a cable chain, put the sensor cable on the opposite edge from the motor pairs.

cable tiesheat shrink
5

Give the motor ground its own wire

  1. Unplug the 12 V supply.
  2. Cut two lengths of 20 AWG (or thicker) black wire.
  3. Solder one from TB6612 #1's GND pin (the one next to VM, where the bulk capacitor from fix 2 goes) straight to a V− screw on the PSU, or to the GND terminal of the 12 V IN plug if the PSU is hard to reach.
  4. Run the second one from TB6612 #2's GND pin to the PSU in the same way. Use another V− screw if one is free.
  5. Leave the ESP32, sensors and XL4015 grounds where they are. They reach the PSU through the buck converter on their own path.
  6. Keep the thin GND wire between the drivers and the ESP32. Once the thick wire is in, it carries almost no current, and the logic signals still need it as a shared reference.

The motor current takes the easiest way back to the supply. With a thick, direct wire, that path no longer runs through the ground the sensor uses, so the sensor's ground stays steady.

20 AWG black wire, 2 runs

Check after each fix

Open the Serial Monitor at 115200 and type noisetest. Each row runs one motor and takes 30 readings from each sensor. You want 0 silent on every row, and ok close to 30.
